Marketing operationsA shared system for tracking and delivering work.

The challenge

Work was tracked inconsistently. Leadership lacked a shared view of priorities and progress, and the team needed a more consistent way to manage incoming work.

My approach

I built a tracking system in Excel and introduced weekly team meetings to establish shared priorities. As needs grew, I helped move the work into Smartsheet and later Asana, supporting adoption at each stage.

What changed

Leadership gained visibility into project status. The team had clearer priorities and a consistent process for taking on and tracking work.

Client relationshipsA long-standing $2M account, restored.

The challenge

A restaurant account was at risk after the client changed agencies, disrupting our existing point of contact. The relationship represented approximately $2M in annual revenue.

My approach

I found the new agency and spent three months building trust through conversations about our history and data expertise. When another vendor failed, I coordinated internally to help resolve the immediate need within a month.

What changed

We restored the relationship and ongoing business. Both the client and the new agency personally expressed their appreciation for the response.

Lifecycle marketingRetention and reactivation beyond acquisition.

The opportunity

A home-services client had focused on new-customer acquisition. Alongside that work, I saw an opportunity to engage people who were already in the customer base.

My approach

I introduced a test focused on upselling existing customers and reactivating inactive ones. The trust built through our ongoing work helped the client feel comfortable exploring the idea.

What changed

The new customer groups outperformed the acquisition groups in the test and opened a retention and reactivation dimension to the client’s marketing strategy.
